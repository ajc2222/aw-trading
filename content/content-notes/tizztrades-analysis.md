# tizztrades — Hook/Retain/Reward Analysis + Editing Style Brief

*Watched: tier-list (259K TikTok), chart recap (47K IG), pattern trade controversy (55K IG), loss recap (44K IG), "What did you take today?" (33K IG), binary question (63K TikTok)*

---

## Hooks Library — Every Type He Uses

### Type 1: Binary Question (63K TikTok)
> *"Is this 10:01, 30 second short a statistical loss or a pattern trade? To find out, watch this video."*

Forces a mental split-decision in second 1. Viewer has to pick a side, which means they're now invested in being right. The explicit "to find out, watch this video" at second 3 is rare for him — he only says it when the question is strong enough to hold the weight.

**AW version:** "Is this an A+ setup or a trap? Watch to the end." / "Did I take this right or did I cost myself money?"

---

### Type 2: Judgment Call / Self-Assessment (recurring, 15K–55K)
> *"Did you take this?" / "Did you take this loss?" / "Did you take or fade?" / "What did you take today?"*

Puts the viewer in the trade emotionally before they've seen a single candle. They have to answer the question in their head — which means they're now committed to watching the answer. Works for wins AND losses equally. The loss version ("Did you take this loss?") is just as strong because of the relatability hook.

**AW versions:**
- "Did you take this?"
- "Did you see this setup?"
- "What would you have done here?"
- "Would you have taken this loss?"
- "Did you fade or ride this?"

---

### Type 3: Named Event (55K IG)
> *"July 17 Judas Incident RECAP"*

First frame is a talking head with a bold white pill title card naming a specific community moment. The hook only works if you were there — but for everyone who was, it's irresistible. Pulls up the other person's Instagram profile on screen within 5 seconds to show follower count as "evidence."

Requires existing community context. Works as an accelerant, not a starting point.

**AW version:** Once you have audience — name a specific day or event ("The July 11 SMT Setup Everyone Missed," "The Trade That Blew Up My Account").

---

### Type 4: Audience Qualifier (44K IG loss)
> *"If you trade ICT..."* (first 3 words on screen, chart already visible)

Immediately filters IN the exact viewer he wants. "If you trade ICT..." tells a beginner this might not be for them — and tells an ICT trader "this is exactly for you." The chart is already on screen before any text appears; the text then qualifies who should care.

Subtypes he uses:
- "If you trade ICT..."
- "If you trade the Mech model / Continuation model..."
- Implied qualifier: showing the chart first (ICT-style annotations immediately signal who the audience is)

**AW versions:**
- "If you trade ICT..."
- "If you're using FVGs to enter..."
- "If you took a loss today..."
- "If you're on a prop firm..."

---

### Type 5: Visual Verdict (33K IG, "Trade of the Day")
> Chart on screen, label reads: **"Trade of The Day ✅"**

No spoken hook needed. The green checkmark on the label does the emotional work before a word is said. Viewer knows immediately: this was a winner. They stay to see *what* it was and *how* it played out. Zero ambiguity — pure dopamine setup.

Used for his cleanest wins. The ✅ emoji is doing as much work as the text.

**AW versions:**
- "Trade of The Day ✅"
- "A+ Setup ✅"
- "This one paid ✅"
- Inverse: "Trade of The Day ❌" (loss version — same curiosity, different emotion)

---

### Type 6: Controversy / Peer Ranking (259K TikTok)
> *"Ranking Trading Creators Based on Free Education"*

Already covered in main analysis. Names peers to bait their audiences into the comments. The hook is the title card on frame 1 — viewers who recognize any name immediately stay to see where they land.

---

### Type 7: Bold Label / Confident Assertion (standard chart recap)
> *"A+ Setup"* on chart, frame 1. No question, no intro.

The most common and replicable hook. He's not asking — he's telling you this was elite. Viewer stays to see *why* he's calling it A+, and to benchmark their own read against his.

Variants seen:
- "A+ Setup"
- "A+ LTF Structure"
- "A+ Setup ✅" (with checkmark = confirmed winner)

## Hook Anatomy — What Every Hook Has

Looking across all videos, every hook he uses hits **at least one** of these three levers:

| Lever | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-assessment** | Forces viewer to evaluate their own behavior | "Did you take this?", "Is this a loss or a pattern trade?" |
| **Social identity** | Signals who this is for / references the in-group | "If you trade ICT...", tier-list naming peers |
| **Verdict** | States a judgment up front, viewer watches to confirm/challenge | "A+ Setup", "Trade of The Day ✅", "This one paid" |

The best hooks hit **two levers at once.** Example: "Did you take this A+ setup?" = self-assessment + verdict. That's the formula to aim for.

---

## Hook Rules He Never Breaks

1. **No intro.** Not once. Never "hey guys," never "what's up." Content starts on frame 1.
2. **The hook is visual first on Instagram** (text on chart), **spoken first on TikTok** (where captions exist). He adapts to platform behavior.
3. **The question is always about the viewer's decision, not the trade itself.** "Did *you* take this" not "here's a trade I took." Viewer = main character.
4. **Loss hooks work as well as win hooks.** The emotion is different (curiosity/sympathy vs. dopamine) but the retention mechanic is identical.
5. **He names the setup type immediately** (A+ Setup, MEC Model, Continuation Model) — this is also a retention device. Viewers who know the model stay; viewers who don't know it now want to learn.

## Hook · Retain · Reward Framework

### Video Type A — Chart Recap (bread & butter, 15K–55K views)

**HOOK (0–3s)**
First frame is already the chart with a bold label on it — no intro, no "hey guys." The question in the caption ("Did you take this?") sets up the viewer as a participant, not a student. They immediately want to know if they would have gotten it right.

**RETAIN (middle)**
Progressive zoom-out → zoom-in. Starts zoomed out at the HTF picture (4h, 1h), labels what most traders would think ("Most Traders 👎 Bad PA/Chop"), then zooms in to show the actual structure they missed. This creates a contrast loop: *wrong belief → right belief* — the viewer stays because they want to close the gap.
- Every ~5–8s a new text card or label appears. The chart is never static.
- "Punctuation cards" (pure white screen, single word like "Meaning" or "However") act as chapter breaks and reset attention.
- Color-coded callouts build layer by layer: HTF context first, then LTF execution, then the specific entry zone.

**REWARD**
The A+ setup confirmation + the actual entry arrow. Viewer gets the dopamine of understanding something they didn't at the start. Ends with either a "follow" CTA or implied one via caption engagement question.

---

### Video Type B — Talking Head + Tier List (outlier, 3–5x baseline)

**HOOK (0–3s)**
Title card overlaid on his face: *"Ranking Trading Creators Based on Free Education"* — no warmup. All the creators' profile pics are already visible in the "waiting" zone above the empty tier rows. Viewer immediately wants to know where their favorite lands.

**RETAIN**
One creator per ~12–15s. Each gets: name pill appearing on screen → quick 2–3 sentence rationale → drop into tier row. The pace is fast enough that leaving would mean missing the next placement. The creator builds manufactured tension by front-loading the controversial one (TJR in F) so viewers stick through the whole list to see if theirs gets better treatment.

**REWARD**
The last creator (Dodgy) is *not* placed — instead a "Where should Dodgy go?" card appears while Dodgy's photo hovers unplaced. This turns the comment section into the reward: viewers must comment to complete the tier list. Every tagged creator then comes to defend themselves, flooding the algorithm with cross-audience engagement.

## Editing Style — Visual Spec

### Chart Recap Format

**Layout:** Portrait 9:16. Pure white background TradingView chart fills ~80–90% of the frame.

**Chart style:**
- White/off-white background (not dark mode)
- Candlesticks: gray/charcoal bodies for bearish, light blue/ice for bullish
- No grid lines, minimal clutter — clean and readable at mobile size

**Text overlays (main narration):**
- Font: Bold sans-serif (Inter or system-sans equivalent), weight 700–900
- Size: Large (~3–4rem equivalent), centered horizontally
- Color: Black on white areas, or white on dark
- Appear and disappear progressively — never all on screen at once
- Examples: "A+ Setup", "Coming into NYAM", "This is A+ LTF Structure", "10:00am Open"

**Annotation overlays (on-chart callouts):**
- FVG zones: Semi-transparent pink/red filled rectangles with red text label (e.g., "4h FVG", "30m FVG", "15m FVG")
- Key structure circles: Blue oval outline drawn around specific candle clusters
- Direction arrows: Bold red downward arrow (for shorts) — large, diagonal, hard to miss
- Horizontal lines: Thin gray lines with small text labels at the right edge (e.g., "4h internal low", "BE", "15m FVG")
- Entry markers: Red ❌ for bad/missed entries, green ✓ for clean ones
- Shaded zones: Light blue or light gray filled rectangles for range/balance areas

**Punctuation cards:**
- Full white screen, single centered text, large weight
- Used between major sections ("To see the execution...", "Meaning", "However 👎")
- Act as a chapter break + re-hook

**Timeframe labels:** Colored inline text to distinguish timeframes — green for bullish TF references, red for bearish, black for neutral

**Chart animation:** Gradual pan/zoom within the still chart (slow drift). The chart "builds" — elements appear one by one rather than all at once.

---

### Talking Head + Graphic Format

**Layout:** Upper ~60% = webcam footage. Lower ~40% = dark background tier graphic.

**Background (talking head portion):**
- Clean, aesthetic home setup: white/cream walls, framed art prints, large fiddle leaf fig plant
- Ring light or similar soft front lighting
- Visible Rolex and blue-glow LED mic (brand signals)

**Outfit:** Casual — white button-up or similar. Nothing distracting.

**Tier graphic (lower half):**
- Dark/black background
- Colored tier rows: S = coral pink, A = orange, B = yellow, C = yellow-green, F = bright green
- Profile picture circles (round, no border) drop into rows as mentioned
- Currently-discussed creator also shown large (zoomed-in circle) above their destination row during the rationale

**Name labels:**
- White rounded-rectangle pill with bold black text
- Appears centered over his head/chest when he mentions that creator
- Disappears once the creator drops into the tier

**Engagement trap:**
- Last creator left floating above tiers, unplaced
- White text card appears: "Where should [name] go?" with pointing gesture from presenter

## Content Structure Templates

### Template 1: Chart Recap (2:00–2:30)

```
[0–3s]   HOOK — Chart already on screen. Bold label: "A+ Setup" or "Did you take this?"
         No intro. Start mid-action.

[3–20s]  CONTEXT — HTF picture. Text: what the bias was, where price was coming from.
         1–2 labeled levels (e.g., "4h internal low", "HTF FVG").

[20–40s] MOST TRADERS READ — Show the choppy/confusing part of price action.
         Text: "Most Traders 👎 [Bad Read]"
         This validates viewer frustration and builds contrast.

[40s–1:10] WHAT WAS ACTUALLY HAPPENING — Zoom in. Layer in real structure.
           Accumulation → Manipulation → Distribution annotations appear one by one.
           Text: "This is A+ [X] Structure"

[1:10–1:40] THE ENTRY — Show the specific trigger zone.
            Red X on the early entry (missed), then circle/label the actual entry.
            "10:22 Short — [Model Name]"
            Red arrow showing direction.

[1:40–2:10] THE EXECUTION / OUTCOME — Chart plays out to target.
            "Price will distribute to the agreed upon DOL"
            Green checkmark on the target hit.

[2:10–2:30] CLOSE — Punctuation card ("Follow for more") or caption CTA.
            "Did you take this?" — leave it open, force engagement.
```

### Template 2: Teaching Concept (1:30–2:00)

```
[0–3s]   HOOK — Counterintuitive statement as text card or spoken.
         "You SHOULD worry about a 4h when you're in a 1h"

[3–20s]  WRONG BELIEF — Chart showing what most traders do wrong.
         "Most traders think [X]"

[20–50s] THE RULE — Explain the concept with chart visuals.
         Each component labeled and appearing sequentially.

[50s–1:20] PROOF — Show a real example where the rule played out.
           Annotated chart with the concept applied.

[1:20–1:30] CLOSE — "Follow for the next setup" or question to comment.
```

### Template 3: Dopamine/Short Clip (0:13–0:20, TikTok-native)

```
[0–3s]   PURE PAYOFF — Best feeling OAT, green P&L, sick entry.
         No context needed. Emotion first.

[3–10s]  THE MOMENT — The chart or the P&L screenshot.
         Minimal text. Let the visual do the work.

[10–18s] RELATABILITY — Caption/text that makes them feel seen.
         "Best feeling OAT‼️" or "1 month vs 1 year into trading"

No CTA needed — short clips build follower reflex, not immediate follows.
```

## HyperFrames / HeyGen Editing Style Brief

*Use this when briefing the video editing tool on how to replicate his style.*

**For chart recap videos (screen-recorded):**
> White background TradingView chart, portrait 9:16. Candlesticks in gray/dark (bearish) and light blue (bullish). Annotations appear sequentially — never all at once. Each annotation type: (1) semi-transparent pink rectangle = FVG zone with small red label, (2) bold red diagonal arrow = short direction, (3) blue oval circle outline = key structure highlight, (4) thin gray horizontal lines with right-edge text labels = levels. Bold black centered text overlays in large sans-serif narrate each phase of the trade, appearing and fading. Between major sections, a pure white "punctuation card" appears with a single centered phrase. Chart has slow pan/zoom animation throughout. No voiceover required — text IS the narration.

> Upper 60% of frame: webcam footage on a clean, minimal, slightly upscale home background (white walls, plant, framed art). Lower 40%: dark background tier-list or similar interactive graphic. When a subject is mentioned, a white rounded-rectangle pill with their name appears centered over presenter's upper body. Profile picture circles animate into graphic rows. Fast pace — new element every 10–15s. End with a deliberate "incomplete" moment (one item left unresolved) that forces comment engagement. Presenter wears casual solid-color top. Soft front lighting.

## Key Takeaways for AW Trading Content

1. **Start on the chart.** No intro, no "hey guys." First frame = first hook.
2. **"Most traders" contrast.** Validate the wrong read before showing the right one. Makes viewers feel smart when they get it.
3. **Layered annotations.** Don't show everything at once. Reveal elements progressively to keep eyes moving.
4. **Punctuation cards work.** Single-word full-white frames reset attention and feel cinematic.
5. **Caption = the real hook.** "Did you take this?" / "Did you take this loss?" puts the viewer in the trade emotionally.
6. **The controversy multiplier** (tier list, beef) only works if you have an existing community to tag. Build audience first, deploy controversy as an accelerant.
7. **Vulnerability converts.** The loss recap ("Did you take this loss?") performs similarly to the win recap — honesty is a retention tool.
